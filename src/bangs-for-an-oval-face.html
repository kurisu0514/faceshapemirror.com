Bangs for an Oval Face: Choose Where the Fringe Falls

Start with brow-skimming bangs if you want more coverage across the middle of your forehead. Choose curtain bangs if you want an open center and longer pieces at the sides, or a long side-swept fringe for diagonal coverage. For a fuller line on fine hair, ask your stylist how they would soften the edge while keeping the fringe looking full.

A blunt line can have a soft edge

In a salon demonstration on an oval face with long, thin hair, a hairstylist starts the blunt fringe longer than the intended finish. To break up the line, the stylist lifts small sections and point-cuts into the back of the fringe. The stylist softens the center and both corners, then checks that the sides match. Cutting demonstration. Checked November 27, 2026.

Curtains may leave more forehead visible than you expect

One wearer with a self-described oval face and a larger forehead tried curtain bangs for coverage. They reported that the fringe covered only a little at the sides. They had medium-length layered hair when they posted and were considering other fringes. Wearer’s account. Checked November 27, 2026.

Midface pieces grow into chin-length layers

Another wearer described a slim oval face and a long-looking midface. They originally chose curtains that reached the middle of the face because they wanted to draw attention away from the forehead. By the time they asked for advice, the pieces had grown to the chin and they were weighing whether to cut them short again. Grow-out account. Checked November 27, 2026.
