Do a mirror check.

Stand at eye level in even light and relax your jaw. Look at the outline of your face from the hairline to the chin.

  1. LengthCompare hairline to chin with the widest part of your face.
  2. WidthCompare your forehead at the temples, your cheekbones and the sides of your jaw.
  3. JawlineNotice whether the sides curve, turn sharply or taper toward your chin.

If your features fit more than one outline, compare both in the guide below.

What do you see?

    Face shapes & haircut ideas.

    Think about what you want the cut to do: add height, create width, or draw attention to the jaw or cheekbones. Use the ideas below as a starting point.

    Oval

    Longer than wide, with gently curved sides and a softly tapered jaw.

    Try soft layers that start around the cheekbones, or a bob that follows the jawline.

    Round

    Length and width are close, with full cheeks and a softly curved jaw.

    Try layers that start below the chin or a little lift at the crown to add a vertical line.

    Square

    Forehead, cheekbones and jaw are close in width, with a defined jaw angle.

    Try soft waves or long, face-framing layers if you want to soften the jawline.

    Heart

    The upper face is broad and the jaw tapers toward a narrower chin.

    Try a chin-length bob or waves that add fullness around the lower half of the face.

    Read our heart face shape hairstyle guide before you choose a cut.

    Considering a fringe? See our guide to bangs for a heart-shaped face.

    Diamond

    The cheekbones stand out as the widest part. The forehead and jaw are narrower.

    Try a side-swept fringe or chin-length layers to bring attention above or below the cheekbones.

    Oblong

    Noticeably longer than wide, often with a forehead and jaw of similar width.

    Try bangs or waves that add width at the sides if you want the face to look shorter.

    What if your jaw is widest?

    A broad jaw with a narrower forehead can be a triangle or pear outline. Try adding fullness at the temples or crown if you want to balance the jaw. Your hair texture, styling routine and preferences matter when you choose the cut.
