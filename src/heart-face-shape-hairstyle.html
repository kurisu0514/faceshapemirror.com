Heart Face Shape Hairstyle: What to Ask For Before You Cut

Heart-shaped faces are “widest at the temple and narrowest at the chin.” Pull your hair back and compare the outline above and below your cheekbones. If the forehead narrows noticeably above the widest part of the cheeks, compare with a diamond shape. Face-shape guide · Forehead and jaw comparison

For a softer chin outline, ask for a jaw-length bob that keeps fullness at the ends. A side-swept fringe can break up the width of the forehead. If you want the chin on show, ask about a cut that ends above it. Jawline fullness · Rounded short cut · Above-chin cut

Our salon-note method

This page’s salon-note method is an editorial aid, not an industry standard. Write down where you want the shortest front pieces to stop and use your cheekbone, jaw or collarbone as the landmark. Note the back length separately. Specify where you want each section to sit when dry. If you wear your hair straight and wavy, tell your stylist which finish should govern the length.

Keep the weight in the ends

For a shorter heart-face cut, a stylist asks for hair that curves “towards the chin,” keeps the perimeter close to one length or limits soft layering to the bottom, and directs beach waves toward the face when styling that short outline. L’Oréal Paris, Haircode and Southern Living discuss jaw-area fullness, but their guides give no matching instruction combining that short cut, layer placement and wave direction. Short sides can leave a soft top

In a heart-face example, a barber describes Nick Jonas’s hair as “very tight on the sides” and high at the sides, with the hair left above them worn soft and textured, and says close sides can work if the top keeps that softness and texture. L’Oréal Paris, Haircode and Southern Living give short-cut ideas, but none explains this specific combination through the barber’s example. We checked the source and those pages on November 26, 2026. Decide how much hair goes into the fringe

For a broad heart-shaped forehead, a stylist says to “bring it close bring it in,” placing the outer edges of the section used for bangs inside the hairline corners so the stylist takes hair from a narrower area before cutting the fringe. L’Oréal Paris, Haircode and Southern Living describe finished fringes without giving this particular instruction for where the section should stop. A hime-bixie case on fine, dense hair

One wearer with a self-described heart-shaped face and straight, fine strands but high density brought their stylist references for hime bobs and bixies, kept their bangs above the eyes, and described the resulting short cut as having longer layers and “little disconnected hime pieces (not too blunt).” L’Oréal Paris, Haircode and Southern Living offer short-cut ideas without this account of combining the two cuts on that hair type. A collarbone cut while the fringe grows

Chris McMillan says he cut Jennifer Aniston’s hair to “clavicle length,” pinned back her still-growing bangs with a bobby pin, let the hair behind them drape over the pin, then gave her a blowout inspired by a deep side part and a flip at the shoulder.
