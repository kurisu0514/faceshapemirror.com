Bangs for Long Oval Face: Coverage, Shape and Hold

Start with brow-skimming bangs if you want to cover the center of your forehead. If your midface still looks long, compare that fringe with curtain bangs and check where the sides land after your hair settles.

When the shape falls out after styling

One wearer described their face as long and oval, with a strong, somewhat square jaw. Their straight hair lay flat on top and fell heavily. They tried curtain bangs to frame the face, but the style fell out about ten minutes after styling. To them, their face then looked even longer. Individual account. Checked November 27, 2026.

When nose-length pieces still leave a concern

Another wearer described a long oval face, a large forehead and a long chin. They had already tried nose-length curtain bangs and layers and were still dissatisfied with how long their face looked. They were considering wispy bangs or curtains that would cover a little more forehead, but had not reported trying either option. Individual account. Checked November 27, 2026.

Two notes to bring to the salon

Write down how much of the center you want covered and where you want the sides to land when dry. This is a Face Shape Mirror planning aid, not an industry standard. For a wider comparison, see our guide to bangs for oval faces.
