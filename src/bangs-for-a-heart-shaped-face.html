Bangs for a Heart Shaped Face: Choose the Width Before You Cut

For a heart-shaped face, start with long side-swept bangs if you want coverage across one temple, or curtain bangs if you want an opening in the middle. If you want a fuller fringe, ask your stylist where the section will stop before you discuss the length. Decide how much forehead you want visible when the bangs are dry.

Set the section before the length

For a wide forehead on a heart-shaped face, a hairstylist keeps the outside edges of the section inside the hairline corners. He treats the section width as the first decision when the goal is to reduce visible forehead width, then leaves room for either a softer fringe or a heavier one. Fringe section demonstration. Choose which feature the fringe frames

Long layered side bangs can cover the outer temple area on a heart-shaped face, while a light, wispy fringe leaves more attention on the curve of the chin. The same styling demonstration cautions that a heavy straight-across fringe can make the whole face look shorter even though it covers more of the forehead. Side bangs, wispy fringe and full fringe. What an asymmetric sweep is like to wear

One wearer with a self-described heart-shaped face and dense, fine hair replaced blunt bangs with a sweep starting near the inner corner of an eye. Hair crossed the forehead on one side and fell like short layers on the other. The wearer liked the coverage but reported that wind displaced the fringe and it did not settle back into place.
